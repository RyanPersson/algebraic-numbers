The algebraic numbers

Every dot is an algebraic number: a root of a polynomial with integer coefficients. Each one is drawn once, using its minimal polynomial (irreducible, coefficients with no common factor, ).

Colour shows the leading coefficient (red = 1: the algebraic integers), or the degree . Size shrinks with the length , so fewer terms and smaller coefficients make bigger dots.

The sizes fall off like , the Dirichlet exponent for approximating a point of the plane by algebraic numbers of degree ( on the real line, on the unit circle). That keeps the density of dots the same at every scale, so you can keep zooming: each view is computed from scratch, by finding every integer polynomial with a root there (lattice reduction + enumeration).

Click a dot to see its minimal polynomial and the ideal it determines, with the maximal ideals above it.

Controls

  • Drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom, double-click to zoom in.
  • Keys: + − zoom, arrows pan, / search, Esc close.
  • V hides all the controls, leaving just the picture. Press it again to bring them back.
  • The address bar keeps the current view, so you can share a link.

Inspired by Stephen J. Brooks’ pictures of the algebraic numbers (by leading coefficient, by degree). Source on GitHub. Zoom is limited only by double-precision arithmetic (about 4·10⁹×).
